## Limits and semantics

GIFs preserve observation boundaries and duration by lowering sampling density when needed. They use up to 220 frames and 96 MiB of RGBA buffers, excluding encoder/source/browser overhead; clips beyond 60 seconds are rejected. The default 560-pixel longest edge preserves aspect ratio. Encoded GIF delay rounding is within roughly one centisecond of target total duration. A play-once GIF omits the infinite-loop extension; viewers may have their own playback policies.

Video is real-time canvas capture, limited to 90 seconds/64 MiB and at most 1,280 pixels on the longest edge. It can deliver fewer frames on slower devices. A hidden tab, cancellation, recorder failure or unexpected early stop rejects the export and releases tracks. No audio is included. MP4/WebM depends on actual browser support and resources; capability detection is not a guarantee of successful recording.

Only the selected period range is rendered; scale/history are recomputed for that range. An explicitly selected history entity can show missing values throughout the range. Captions retain the chart's two-line visual limit; full interactive text is available in HTML instead. Video/GIF have no tooltips, controls or source-data tables. Chart settings are snapshotted at job start and are not rewritten.
